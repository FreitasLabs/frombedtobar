Recipes

Seasonal cocktails and mocktails.

Peace in a Glass

Lavender changes the pace of a drink.

It softens lemonade.

It settles into tea.

It brings quiet elegance to sparkling water.

It gives cocktails and mocktails a gentle floral note without asking to be the center of attention.

That is why we make lavender syrup.

Not because it is complicated.

Because it is simple.

Because it helps carry a little of the garden into the softer parts of the day — the porch moments, the after-dinner tea, the glass poured while the light is fading and the house finally begins to settle.

The secret is restraint.

Lavender should feel like ease in the background, not perfume in the glass.

Less is more.

The Recipe

Simple by design.

This syrup is meant to stay simple. Water, sugar, and a little culinary lavender are enough. The real work is restraint — tasting as it steeps and stopping while the flavor is still soft.

What We Use

  • 1 cup water
  • 1 cup sugar
  • 1 tablespoon dried culinary lavender buds

How We Make It

  1. Add the water and sugar to a small saucepan.
  2. Warm gently over medium heat, stirring until the sugar dissolves.
  3. Remove from heat and stir in the lavender.
  4. Let steep for 10 to 15 minutes.
  5. Taste as it steeps. When the flavor is soft and floral, strain out the lavender.
  6. Let cool, then store in a clean jar or bottle in the refrigerator.

A Small Garden Note

Lavender can become strong quickly, especially once dried.

Start with less.

Taste often.

Stop steeping before the flavor becomes sharp or soapy.

The goal is balance, not intensity.

Ways We Enjoy It

The gentler kinds of drinks.

We reach for lavender syrup when a drink needs a softer edge, not a louder flavor.

A spoonful stirred into lemonade on a warm afternoon.

A little added to hot tea after dinner.

A splash in sparkling water when we want something pretty but simple.

It is beautiful in mocktails with lemon, honey, cucumber, or mint.

It also belongs in gentle cocktails where the lavender can remain in the background — gin, vodka, lemon, bubbles, or a light herbal spritz.

But our favorite way to enjoy it is slowly.

Not poured heavily.

Not hidden beneath too many flavors.

Just enough to make the glass feel settled.

Garden to Glass

Carrying the garden forward.

Lavender begins in the garden as something beautiful before it ever becomes something useful.

The flowers bring color, bees, fragrance, and a sense of pause. Then, when the stems are cut and dried, they become a way to hold onto summer.

A small jar of dried lavender buds on the shelf can become tea in winter, lemonade in spring, or syrup for evening glasses long after the garden has gone bare.

FAQ

Lavender Simple Syrup Questions

Can I use fresh lavender instead of dried lavender?

Yes. Dried culinary lavender is easier to control and store, but fresh lavender can be used as well. The flavor will simply be lighter and more delicate.

How long should lavender steep in simple syrup?

Start tasting around 10 minutes. Most batches only need 10 to 15 minutes. Longer steeping can make the syrup taste too strong or perfume-like.

What kind of lavender should I use?

Use culinary lavender. Avoid plants treated with pesticides or lavender intended for fragrance products rather than food.

How long does lavender simple syrup last?

Stored in a clean jar or bottle in the refrigerator, lavender simple syrup usually keeps for about two weeks.

What drinks go well with lavender syrup?

Lavender syrup works beautifully in lemonade, iced tea, hot tea, sparkling water, mocktails, gin cocktails, vodka drinks, and light citrus spritzes.
