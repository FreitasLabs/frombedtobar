The Recipe

Simple by design.

This syrup is meant to stay simple. Water, sugar, and a handful of fresh basil are enough. The real work is stopping while the basil still tastes bright, green, and alive.

What We Use

  • 1 cup water
  • 1 cup sugar
  • 1/2 cup loosely packed fresh basil leaves

How We Make It

  1. Add the water and sugar to a small saucepan.
  2. Warm gently over medium heat, stirring until the sugar dissolves.
  3. Remove from heat and stir in the fresh basil leaves.
  4. Let steep for 20 to 30 minutes, tasting as the flavor develops.
  5. When the syrup tastes bright and fresh, strain out the leaves before it turns grassy.
  6. Let cool, then store in a clean jar or bottle in the refrigerator.

A Small Garden Note

Basil is best when it still tastes fresh.

Do not boil the leaves.

Use gentle heat.

Taste often.

Stop steeping before the syrup turns grassy or overly herbal.

The goal is summer brightness, not intensity.

Water. Sugar. A handful of basil. That is enough.

FAQ

Basil Simple Syrup Questions

Can I use dried basil instead of fresh basil?

Fresh basil is strongly recommended. Dried basil tastes flatter and more savory, and it does not capture the bright summer flavor that makes basil syrup useful in drinks.

How long should basil steep in simple syrup?

Start tasting around 20 minutes. Most batches are ready between 20 and 30 minutes. Longer steeping can make the syrup taste grassy or overly herbal.

What kind of basil should I use?

Genovese or sweet basil is the most versatile. Thai basil can be used for a spicier, anise-forward syrup, but it will change the flavor profile.

How long does basil simple syrup last?

Stored in a clean jar or bottle in the refrigerator, basil simple syrup usually keeps for about two weeks.

What drinks go well with basil syrup?

Basil syrup works beautifully in lemonade, sparkling water, iced tea, mocktails, gin drinks, vodka drinks, tequila cocktails, and summer spritzes.

Does basil syrup work in lemonade?

Yes. Lemonade may be the best place to start. Add a small splash, taste, and add more if you want the basil to come forward.

Can basil syrup be frozen?

Yes. Freeze it in ice cube trays, then move the cubes to a freezer bag or container. The cubes are useful for lemonade, iced tea, mocktails, and quick summer drinks later.
